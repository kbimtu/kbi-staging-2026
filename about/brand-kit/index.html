One line

KBI is a non-profit that builds communities applying practical STEM to solve real-world problems.

Short

The Königsberger Bridges Institute (KBI) is a non-profit founded in 2017 and now registered in Estonia. It builds communities of competent, curious, kind, and fun people who apply practical STEM to solve real-world problems. KBI runs the International KBI Olympiads (I2OL) — IBCOL in blockchain, IDSOL in data science, and IQCOL in quantum computing — and the Emerging Technology Olympiads (ETO) combined meet competitions.

IBCOL

The International Blockchain Olympiad (IBCOL) is an annual global competition where young people apply blockchain and trust technologies to real-world challenges, themed around the UN Sustainable Development Goals.

IDSOL

The International Data Science Olympiad (IDSOL) is an annual global competition where young people apply data science and AI to real-world challenges, themed around the UN Sustainable Development Goals.

IQCOL

The International Quantum Computing Olympiad (IQCOL) is an annual global competition where young people apply quantum computing and information theory to real-world challenges, themed around the UN Sustainable Development Goals.

ETO

The Emerging Technology Olympiads (ETO) are the combined meet competitions for quality and qualified projects from the three KBI Olympiads (IBCOL, IDSOL, IQCOL) to find the most promising projects for further commercial development through research or investment. ETO East focuses on the backstage aspects and infrastructures of innovation and technology, such as supply chain, logistics, trade, financing, manufacturing, and rapid prototyping. ETO West focuses on tech sovereignty, such as data privacy, geopolitical hedging, cybersecurity, open source, freedom vs responsibility, and global governance and regulations.

Long

Mission and approach

The Königsberger Bridges Institute (KBI) is a non-profit founded in 2017 and now registered in Estonia. Its vision is to prepare humanity for unknown unknowns through continuous innovation. Its mission is to build communities of competent, curious, kind, and fun people, who uplift each other and society towards that vision.

KBI takes its name from the Königsberger Bridges Problem. In 1736, Leonhard Euler answered it with mathematics: a fun question from everyday life, solved with practical STEM. KBI works the same way, bridging tech with design thinking, ambitions to actions, cultural and social divides, and academia and industry.

Educational framework

Through the International KBI Olympiads (I2OL) — the International Blockchain Olympiad (IBCOL), the International Data Science Olympiad (IDSOL), and the International Quantum Computing Olympiad (IQCOL) — young people design solutions to real-world problems from first principles and bring their best work to academic and industry experts from around the world. KBI also offers competency assurance for market-ready, in-demand skills.

Community

The KBI community meets at the Emerging Technology Olympiads (ETO): the ETO Eastern Conference in Hong Kong and Shenzhen and the ETO Western Conference in Oxford and London. Members also contribute through internships and KBI Working Groups, and take part in Emerging Research and Tech Weeks and Socratic Circles.
